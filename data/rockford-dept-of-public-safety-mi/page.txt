Overview

Rockford Dept of Public Safety MI uses Flock Safety technology to capture objective evidence without compromising on individual privacy. Rockford Dept of Public Safety MI utilizes retroactive search to solve crimes after they've occurred. Additionally, Rockford Dept of Public Safety MI utilizes real time alerting of hotlist vehicles to capture wanted criminals, stolen vehicles, and missing persons. In an effort to ensure proper usage and guardrails are in place, they have made the below policies and usage statistics available to the public.

What ALPR Captures, How Information Is Retained, and What an Observation Does Not Establish

The Rockford Department of Public Safety uses automated license plate recognition (ALPR) technology to capture vehicle-related information from vehicles passing by an ALPR camera.

An ALPR observation may include:

- The license plate number
- An image of the vehicle and the license plate
- The date and time of the observation
- The location of the ALPR camera
- General visible vehicle characteristics, such as make, color, body style, and other distinguishing features

ALPR technology provides a time and location-based observation of a vehicle. It does not independently identify the driver or passengers of the vehicle, nor does it establish that a crime has occurred or that the vehicle or its occupants are involved in any criminal activity.

An ALPR observation does not include:

- Facial recognition
- Any personal identifying information
- Recognition of race, gender, or personal identity of vehicle occupants
- Sound or audio surveillance

An ALPR alert or search result serves as an investigative lead. It must be evaluated alongside other available information and verified independently before any enforcement action is taken. Personnel must ensure they have the appropriate lawful basis for any investigative detention, search, arrest, or other law enforcement actions.

The standard retention period for ALPR observation data at RDPS is 30 days. Information may be retained beyond this period only if it is relevant to an authorized investigation, preserved as evidence, required by court order, or mandated by law. Evidence that is preserved is maintained in accordance with applicable evidence retention, records retention, and legal requirements.

Access to ALPR information is restricted to trained and authorized personnel. Searches and user activities are logged and may be reviewed to ensure compliance with RDPS policy, applicable laws, and authorized law enforcement purposes. All data is captured by the ALPR is owned by RDPS.

Authorized Use Policy

Authorized uses of ALPR information include:

- Investigating reported criminal activity
- Identifying or locating vehicles related to a criminal investigation
- Locating stolen vehicles or stolen license plates
- Assisting in the identification or recovery of stolen property
- Locating vehicles associated with individuals wanted for criminal activity or with valid arrest warrants
- Assisting in locating missing, endangered, or abducted persons
- Responding to serious or imminent threats to public safety
- Developing, confirming, or corroborating investigative leads
- Identifying possible connections between related criminal incidents
- Supporting authorized investigations conducted with other law enforcement or criminal justice agencies
- Other lawful criminal justice purposes as authorized by RDPS policy

Data is used for law enforcement purposes only. Data is owned by Rockford Dept of Public Safety MI and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Prohibited Uses

- Personal Use.
- Immigration enforcement.
- Traffic enforcement.
- Harassment or intimidation of any individual or group, based solely on a protected class (i.e. race, religion, gender, ethnicity, nationality, sexual orientation, political affiliation, disability, or other classification protected by law).
- For the sole purpose of identifying any individual or group in the exercise of First Amendment rights.

Access, Audit, and Search Policy

- All system access requires a valid law enforcement reason.
- All system searches require an offense type, police incident number, and a reason for the search.
- All system activity is routinely audited.
- All system access is stored indefinitely.

Hotlist Policy

Myth vs. Fact
Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
