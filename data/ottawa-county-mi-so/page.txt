Overview

Ottawa County MI SO uses Flock Safety technology to capture objective evidence without compromising on individual privacy. Ottawa County MI SO utilizes retroactive searches to solve crimes after they've occurred. Additionally, Ottawa County MI SO utilizes real-time alerting of hotlist vehicles to capture wanted criminals, stolen vehicles, and missing citizens. In an effort to ensure proper usage and guardrails are in place, they have made the policies and usage statistics below available to the public.

Policy & Trust

What's Detected

ALPR cameras capture the following information as a vehicle passes within view of a camera:
● An image of the vehicle's rear license plate
● General vehicle characteristics, including make, type, and color
● The date, time, and location of the capture

What's Not Detected

ALPR cameras do not capture or perform the following:
● Facial recognition or identification of drivers or passengers
● Images of a vehicle's interior
● Biometric data of any kind
● Audio recording
● Continuous, real time tracking of a specific vehicle absent a search connected to a case

Acceptable Use Policy

Access to ALPR data is restricted to trained and authorized OCSO personnel and is limited to legitimate law enforcement purposes, including:
● Investigating a specific criminal offense
● Locating stolen vehicles
● Locating wanted or missing persons
● Responding to an automated alert generated through LEIN, NCIC, or a Flock hotlist match
● Sharing with other law enforcement agencies in accordance with OCSO Policy 424
Every search of the system is logged with the user, the reason for the search, and the case to which it is connected. An alert or a plate match is one piece of information; deputies must independently establish reasonable suspicion or probable cause before taking any enforcement action.
Data is owned by Ottawa County MI SO and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Prohibited Uses

The following uses of ALPR data are prohibited under OCSO policy:
● Personal use unrelated to an official law enforcement function
● Harassment or intimidation of any individual
● Use based solely on a protected characteristic, such as race, sex, or religion
Any employee found to have violated this policy is subject to disciplinary action.

Policy 424 ALPR Policy

424.1 Purpose and Scope
The purpose of this policy is to provide guidance for the storage, use, and sharing of digital data obtained through the use of Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) technology.

424.2 Policy
The policy of the Ottawa County Sheriff's Office is to utilize ALPR technology to capture and store digital license plate data and images while recognizing the established privacy rights of the public. All data and images gathered by the ALPR are for the official use of this office. Because such data may contain confidential information, it is not open to public review.

424.3 Administration
ALPR technology, also known as License Plate Recognition (LPR), allows for the automated detection of license plates. It is used by OCSO to convert data associated with vehicle license plates for official law enforcement purposes, including identifying stolen or wanted vehicles, stolen license plates, and missing or endangered persons. It may also be used to gather information related to active warrants, electronic surveillance, suspect interdiction, and stolen property recovery.
All installation and maintenance of ALPR equipment, as well as ALPR data retention and access, is managed by the Investigative Services Captain, who assigns members to administer the day-to-day operation of the ALPR equipment and data.

424.4 Operations
Use of an ALPR is restricted to the purposes below. Office members shall not use, or allow others to use, the equipment or database records for any unauthorized purpose.
● An ALPR shall only be used for official law enforcement business.
● An ALPR may be used in conjunction with any routine patrol operation or criminal investigation. Reasonable suspicion or probable cause is not required before using an ALPR.
● While an ALPR may be used to canvass license plates around any crime scene, particular consideration should be given to using ALPR-equipped vehicles to canvass areas around homicides, shootings, and other major incidents. Partial license plates reported during major crimes should be entered into the ALPR system in an attempt to identify suspect vehicles.
● No member of this office shall operate ALPR equipment or access ALPR data without first completing office-approved training.
● No ALPR operator may access confidential office, state, or federal data unless authorized to do so.
● If practicable, the deputy shall verify an ALPR response through the appropriate official law enforcement database (LEIN, NCIC, SOS) before taking enforcement action that is based solely on an ALPR alert.
● All searches of an ALPR system shall include a reason for the search and the case/CAD number.
● Searches related to broadcasts by dispatch that do not have a case/CAD number, such as suspected drivers or vehicles wanted by other jurisdictions, will include "Broadcast" and the reason (for example, "Broadcast, GRPD Shooting Suspect Vehicle").

When notified of an NCIC hit from an ALPR system, officers shall verify the response through LEIN before taking enforcement action; action shall not be based solely on an ALPR alert. Occasionally there may be errors in an ALPR system's reading of a license plate, so an alert alone is never a basis for police action.

Prior to initiating a stop or other intervention based on an alert, officers shall:
● Verify the status on the hot list. An officer must confirm the vehicle is still stolen, wanted, or otherwise of interest before proceeding, absent exigent circumstances. Without confirmation, deputies cannot stop a vehicle solely on an ALPR alert, and must have independent legal justification for the stop, such as a traffic violation, reasonable suspicion, or probable cause.
● Visually verify the license plate number. Officers shall visually confirm that the plate of interest matches the ALPR-captured image, including the alphanumeric characters, state of issue, and vehicle descriptors, before proceeding.
● Recognize that an alert relates to a vehicle plate, not necessarily the person operating it. Officers need independent reasonable suspicion or probable cause to make an enforcement stop; where a vehicle is entered into the system because of its association with a wanted individual, officers shall attempt to visually match the occupant to that individual's description, or have another legal basis for the stop.

424.5 Data Collection and Retention
The Investigative Services Captain is responsible for ensuring systems and processes are in place for the proper collection and retention of ALPR data. Data is transferred from vehicles to designated storage in accordance with office procedures. All stored ALPR data is retained in accordance with the established records retention schedule; thereafter, ALPR data is purged unless it has become, or it is reasonable to believe it will become, evidence in a criminal or civil action, or is subject to a discovery request or other lawful action to produce records. In those circumstances, the applicable data is downloaded onto Evidence.com and stored digitally.

424.6 Accountability
All data is closely safeguarded and protected by both procedural and technological means. OCSO observes the following safeguards:
● All ALPR data downloaded to the mobile workstation and in storage is accessible only through a login/password-protected system capable of documenting all access to information by name, date, and time.
● Members approved to access ALPR data are permitted to do so for legitimate law enforcement purposes only, such as a specific criminal investigation or office-related civil or administrative action.
● ALPR system audits are conducted regularly by the Professional Standards Captain or other assigned system administrator.

424.7 Releasing ALPR Data
ALPR data may be shared only with other law enforcement or prosecutorial agencies for official law enforcement purposes, or as otherwise permitted by law, using the following procedures:
● ALPR data may be shared with the Prosecutor's Office in accordance with other digital evidence sharing procedures.
● The requesting agency makes a written request that includes the name of the agency, the name of the requesting person, and the intended purpose of obtaining the information.
● The request is reviewed by the Investigative Services Captain or an authorized designee and approved before it is fulfilled.
● The approved request is retained on file.
Requests for ALPR data by non-law enforcement or non-prosecutorial agencies are processed as provided in the Records Maintenance and Release Policy.

424.8 ALPR Network Data Sharing
A. Network Discoverability and Access Preferences. To facilitate multi-jurisdictional cooperation while maintaining data security, the ALPR network presence is configured as follows: the OCSO ALPR network is categorized as a "discoverable network" nationwide, allowing other law enforcement agencies to identify the network and formally request data access; approved external agencies are granted both historical search capabilities and real-time alert functions within the system.
B. Geographic Sharing Parameters. Sharing is controlled by geographic boundaries and agency-type designation. State of Michigan agencies (local, county, and state law enforcement) receive automated acceptance of incoming sharing requests; federal agencies are explicitly excluded from this automated acceptance. Camera data is also actively shared with approved law enforcement agencies within a 500-mile radius of Ottawa County under the same regional sharing tier; federal organizations are explicitly excluded from this tier as well.
C. Data Governance and Restrictions. All shared ALPR data is designated "For Official Use Only" and must be used for legitimate law enforcement and public safety purposes. Data may only be shared with official law enforcement or prosecutorial agencies. The Investigative Services Captain, or a designated system administrator, retains authority to manage, audit, and revoke network sharing access to ensure ongoing policy compliance. Urgent or ad hoc requests from agencies outside the automated sharing parameters must be reviewed and approved by the Investigative Services Captain or an authorized designee before fulfillment.
D. Data Sharing Preferences. The Sheriff's Office reserves the right to determine what offense-type data will be shared with other law enforcement agencies. Shared data is limited to major crimes and public safety-related matters only; examples of data not shared include traffic analysis and civil traffic offenses.

Myth vs. Fact
Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
