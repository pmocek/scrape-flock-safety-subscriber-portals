Overview

The Arvada Police Department utilizes Flock Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) cameras to support criminal investigations and enhance community safety. These stationary cameras only capture still images of license plates as well as key vehicle characteristics, including color, make, and unique identifying features such as bumper/window stickers, top/back racks, or tool boxes. This information is often crucial when suspects remove or alter license plates to avoid detection. By allowing investigators to search for distinctive vehicle traits, ALPR technology helps us more efficiently identify suspects, recover stolen vehicles, and solve crimes, often without escalating encounters or putting the public at additional risk.


The Arvada Police Department is committed to protecting individual privacy and ensuring all technology is used lawfully and responsibly. Our ALPR practices operate within clear constitutional boundaries and are guided by long-standing principles of public safety, accountability, and transparency. In accordance with department policy, all Flock data and images are automatically deleted after 30 days, unless they are preserved as evidence in an active investigation. This limited retention period ensures an appropriate balance between investigative effectiveness and privacy protections.

The summaries below provide real examples of how ALPR data has contributed to arrests, vehicle recoveries and investigative breakthroughs, including violent crime cases. These examples also demonstrate how the system is used responsibly, including instances where alerts helped exonerate someone and prevent unnecessary enforcement action.


Cases are listed in chronological order, beginning with the most recent.

AR26002067 | Jan. 28, 2026 | Suspicious Incident

Police received a report that an individual was seen dragging what appeared to be a person into a minivan. Officers responded but were unable to locate the vehicle. During an area canvass, investigators obtained surveillance video showing a white male exiting a gray minivan on West 57th Avenue. The male placed a backpack on a bench inside Wolff Park, returned to the vehicle, then appeared to drag an unconscious or possibly deceased person to the rear and opposite side of the minivan before placing the individual back inside and driving westbound.

Based on the video, the situation appeared extremely concerning and potentially involved a homicide. Investigators searched Flock ALPR cameras in the area and located the minivan. Using the captured license plate, officers contacted the registered owner and determined the driver had picked up a family member experiencing substance abuse issues and initially wanted to drop him in the park to sleep. The driver then became concerned and transported the family member to a hospital for medical attention, which investigators confirmed with hospital staff.

Impact: ALPR technology allowed investigators to quickly identify the vehicle and determine that no crime had occurred. Without ALPR data, the incident could have remained an active and resource-intensive investigation involving a suspected body disposal.

Policy & Trust

Acceptable use policy

All ALPR data and images are used solely for official law enforcement purposes, including preventing and investigating crime. Because this information may contain sensitive details, it is not available for public review. All data is owned by the Arvada Police Department and is never sold to any third party.

Prohibited uses

Immigration enforcement, traffic enforcement, harassment or intimidation, usage based solely on a protected class (i.e. race, sex, religion), personal use, or for any other use prohibited by related Department policies and/or state and federal laws.

Access policy

Persons approved to access ALPR data under Department policy are permitted to access the data for legitimate law enforcement purposes only, such as when the data relate to a specific criminal investigation or department-related civil or administrative action. ALPR system audits are conducted on a regular basis. Such audits will ensure that the ALPR system is used only by authorized personnel, and that the ALPR data is administered, managed, and stored in compliance with all applicable security and authentication requirements and applicable law.


Hotlist policy

Hotlists are databases of license plates linked to stolen vehicles, wanted persons, AMBER Alerts, and other law-enforcement alerts. They may also include locally created lists or plates manually added by authorized users for active investigations. Hotlists help officers identify vehicles connected to crimes or safety concerns and support both investigative work and officer safety. Hotlist leads must be verified by a human prior to any official actions.
