Overview

Lucas County OH SO uses Flock Safety technology to capture objective evidence without compromising on individual privacy. Lucas County OH SO utilizes retroactive search to solve crimes after they've occurred. Additionally, Lucas County OH SO utilizes real time alerting of hotlist vehicles to capture wanted criminals. In an effort to ensure proper usage and guardrails are in place, they have made the below policies and usage statistics available to the public.

Community Impact
Featured Success Stories

Suspect Vehicle Located After Pursuit

September 2023: The Flock camera system played a significant role in advancing an investigation by providing multiple license plate detections of a suspect Jeep after an initial pursuit. Although the vehicle was not located immediately, subsequent Flock camera hits documented its movements at five separate locations throughout the day, establishing its direction of travel and placing it near the listed residence of the renter. This information allowed deputies to initiate follow-up investigative actions, coordinate with the Toledo Police Department, and continue monitoring the vehicle’s movements. Additional Flock alerts later in the day ultimately led to the successful location of the Jeep, allowing deputies to conduct an investigatory traffic stop and identify the driver.


Stolen Vehicle Recovered

November 2023: The Lucas County Sheriff’s Office successfully recovered a stolen Chrysler 300 and detained multiple occupants after the Flock Safety camera system provided real-time intelligence that guided deputies directly to the vehicle. The investigation began when a Flock alert identified the stolen vehicle entering Lucas County. As the vehicle continued traveling through Springfield Township and Holland, additional Flock camera detections tracked its movements across multiple locations, allowing deputies to monitor its route and strategically position themselves ahead of it. Using this information, deputies located the vehicle in a retail parking lot, where the driver fled, leading to a pursuit that ended with the recovery of the stolen vehicle and the detention of multiple occupants, including a juvenile.

Fugitive Arrest Leads to Homicide Investigation

August 2023: The Northern Ohio Violent Fugitive Task Force used a Flock Safety alert to locate a subject wanted for a parole violation. Officers located the subject driving the identified vehicle and conducted a traffic stop, taking the individual into custody without incident. During the booking process, investigators received information that the subject might be involved in a homicide, prompting further investigation. Officers responded to a residence where they discovered a deceased victim. The investigation ultimately resulted in a murder conviction and a life sentence.

Felony Failure-to-Comply Investigation

May 2024: Deputies utilized Flock camera technology to identify a Chevrolet Silverado associated with a subject known to have a suspended driver’s license and whose vehicle had recently been involved in a prior failure-to-comply pursuit. Based on the Flock alert and vehicle information, a deputy positioned himself in the likely travel area and located the vehicle. When deputies attempted a traffic stop, the driver fled at high speed through multiple roads, fields, construction sites, and residential properties before the pursuit was terminated for public safety reasons. Flock provided timely vehicle location information that helped deputies identify the suspect vehicle, anticipate its direction of travel, locate it quickly, and connect it to prior criminal activity, supporting the filing of a felony failure-to-comply warrant.

Stolen Vehicle and Suspects Located

July 2025: A Flock Safety alert indicated that a Hyundai Elantra had been reported stolen in the Airport Highway area. An officer located the vehicle occupied by two individuals and followed it until backup arrived. The suspects eventually abandoned the vehicle and fled on foot. Officers established a perimeter and later located both suspects attempting to blend in at a nearby business. A K9 search recovered additional evidence, and both suspects were taken into custody and booked on several felony and misdemeanor charges. The stolen vehicle was ultimately returned to its owner.

Stolen Vehicle Alert Leads to Arrest

August 2025: A Flock Safety alert identified a stolen Ford Escape in the area of McCord and Angola. Officers followed the vehicle through several roads and parking areas until the driver eventually stopped after attempts to evade law enforcement. During the arrest, the suspect resisted commands, causing minor injuries to officers. Multiple identification cards and items belonging to other individuals were recovered from the vehicle. The suspect was also found to have outstanding warrants, and suspected narcotics were located during the booking process. The stolen vehicle was returned to its owner.

Violent Felony Warrant Arrest

October 2025: An officer received a Flock alert involving a vehicle connected to a violent felony warrant. Officers positioned themselves along the vehicle’s anticipated route and conducted a felony traffic stop. The wanted individual was taken into custody without incident and transported to the Lucas County Corrections Center for processing.

Multiple Warrants and Illegal Drugsry

October 2025: A Flock alert identified a Chrysler 300 associated with felony warrants. Officers located the vehicle and conducted a traffic stop, ultimately detaining multiple occupants after confirming their identities and warrants. The stop resulted in the recovery of numerous drugs and paraphernalia.

Human and Labor Trafficking Investigation

November 2025: Task Force Detectives received a Flock Custom Hot List Alert regarding a van associated with an ongoing human and labor trafficking investigation. The vehicle was known to travel between Chicago and New York and had previously been connected to suspicious activity. Detectives conducted a traffic stop and discovered multiple occupants carrying significant amounts of currency, including money concealed within personal bags. The vehicle’s travel pattern, known associations, and circumstances surrounding the concealed currency provided additional intelligence in the ongoing investigation.

Identity Theft and Fraud Investigation

June 2026: Investigators used Flock camera data to track a rented U-Haul associated with suspects involved in fraudulent purchases using stolen virtual credit cards. Flock data helped investigators locate the vehicle and conduct a traffic stop, allowing detectives to identify individuals connected to the identity theft ring. Investigators recovered evidence linking the suspects to fraudulent purchases at multiple retailers using stolen credit card information.

Indecent Exposure Investigation

June 2026: Flock Safety camera technology was used to identify and track a subject suspected of repeatedly exposing himself to customers at retail stores throughout Northwest Ohio. Flock data helped investigators track the suspect’s movements to the Perrysburg area and provide the appropriate local law enforcement agency with information regarding the subject’s identity and location. The investigation also determined that the suspect was from the Cleveland area, providing additional intelligence to partner agencies.

Patrol Vehicle Vandalism Investigation

July 2026: Lucas County Sheriff’s Office vehicles were vandalized on two separate occasions while parked at a local business. During the second incident, all four tires on a patrol vehicle were punctured, and graffiti was spray-painted on the vehicle. Detectives utilized Flock camera technology to track the suspect’s movements and gather additional investigative information. The suspect was ultimately located, detained, and arrested, and electronic devices were seized as evidence for further investigation.

Missing Juvenile Located Safely

July 2026: A missing Ohio juvenile was located safely after deputies used Flock license plate reader cameras to locate the suspect vehicle in less than 30 minutes. After identifying the vehicle’s travel pattern and receiving a real-time alert, deputies intercepted the vehicle in Lucas County. The missing juvenile was located inside the vehicle, and an adult suspect was detained and found to be carrying a loaded handgun. The suspect was turned over to the lead investigating agency.

Data is used for law enforcement purposes only. Data is owned by Lucas County OH SO and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Myth vs. Fact
Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
