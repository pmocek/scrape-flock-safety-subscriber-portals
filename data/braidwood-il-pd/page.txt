Overview

Braidwood IL PD uses Flock Safety technology to capture objective evidence without compromising on individual privacy. Braidwood IL PD utilizes retroactive search to solve crimes after they've occurred. Additionally, Braidwood IL PD utilizes real time alerting of hotlist vehicles to capture wanted criminals. In an effort to ensure proper usage and guardrails are in place, they have made the below policies and usage statistics available to the public.

May 2026 Success Story #1

On May 3, BP Brake Time Gas Station in Braidwood was burglarized in the middle of the night by four individuals who stole cartons of cigarettes.

With no witnesses and limited physical evidence present, The Braidwood Police Department utilized Flock license plate reader technology to identify and locate the suspect vehicle. Investigators were able to track the vehicle’s movements to the north side of Chicago, where the vehicle was subsequently recovered and towed.

The investigation led to the identification of a suspect, who was ultimately charged in connection with the burglary of the Braidwood gas station.

Flock technology provided Braidwood PD with a critical investigative lead, helping officers locate the suspect vehicle and move the case toward a successful resolution.

May 2026 Success Story #2

On May 16 WESCOM (dispatch) broadcast information from the Benton County Arkansas Sheriff's Department (BCSD) regarding a vehicle wanted in connection with a missing and endangered 14 year old out of their county.

The BCSD determined the juvenile victim rode with a 24 year old female suspect northbound Interstate 55 towards the Bolingbrook area. The BCSD provided the suspects vehicle description and license plate information. Additionally, the BCSD obtained an Arkansas arrest warrant for the suspect, equivalent to Kidnapping under the Illinois Criminal Code.

Braidwood PD Officers accessed the FLOCK system and learned the vehicle the BCSD believed the suspect was in was currently in Wisconsin, not traveling on Interstate 55 northbound in Illinois. Braidwood PD Officers then used several law enforcement databases available to them and searched for any vehicles associated to the suspect. The Officers ran those associated vehicles against Flock LPR's and located the correct suspect vehicle being a 2015 Chevrolet Spark. Investigators from BCSD were tracking the victims phone, which corroborated the Chevrolet Sparks movements with the victim.

A short time later, Braidwood PD located the suspect vehicle traveling northbound in Interstate 55 and conducted a traffic stop on it. The suspect was placed under arrest and the 14 year old victim was taken into protective custody.

The suspect met the victim on Tik Tok live the year prior and manipulated her over the following 11 months; culminating in the suspects attempt to permanently remove the victim from her Arkansas home and bring her to the Chicagoland area. The suspect ultimately was charged with 7 felony charges including Internet Stalking of a Child and Transportation of Minors for Prohibited Sexual Conduct.

The offender slipped by countless local, county, and state agencies along her 572 mile route from Arkansas to suburban Chicago.

Flock provided Braidwood PD with a critical investigative lead that helped officers identify the suspect vehicle, overcome conflicting location information, and safely recover the endangered 14-year-old victim.

Policy & Trust

What's Detected

License Plates, Vehicles

What's Not Detected

Facial recognition, People, Gender, Race

Acceptable Use Policy

Data is used for law enforcement purposes only. Data is owned by Braidwood IL PD and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Prohibited Uses

Immigration enforcement, harassment or intimidation, personal use and usage based solely on a protected class (i.e. race, sex, religion), Personal use.

Access Policy

All system access requires a valid reason and is stored indefinitely. While the data associated with a search is only retained for 30 days, the search itself is retained indefinitely. This search retention is in place for accountability purposes.

Hotlist Policy

Hotlist hits are required to be human verified prior to taking enforcement action.

Myth vs. Fact
Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
