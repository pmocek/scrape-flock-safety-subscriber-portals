Overview

The Grandville Police Department has remained transparent in its use of Flock Safety technology since its approval during an open city council meeting on June 10, 2024. Launching this transparency portal represents another step in our ongoing commitment to openness and accountability. This technology assists us in deterring criminal behavior, assisting in criminal investigations, and prosecutions by capturing objective evidence (license plates and vehicle characteristics) while maintaining strong safeguards that protect individual privacy. The Grandville Police Department is committed to providing the highest level of service to victims of crime by utilizing all available and appropriate technology to support thorough, efficient, and effective criminal investigations.

We use retroactive searches to support investigations after crimes have occurred, and we also rely on real-time information alerts from the National Center for Missing and Endangered Children, AMBER Alerts, and the Michigan Law Enforcement Information Network (LEIN) to include stolen vehicles, and missing persons. Currently, our cameras are deployed within our southern retail corridor.

Because criminal activity frequently crosses city, county, and state boundaries, we work in partnership with other law enforcement agencies. We provide access to our system when appropriate and request reciprocal access to their systems to support investigations involving our suspects.

Featured Success Story

The Flock system is beneficial in assisting the Grandville Police in solving many crimes. Below are just some of the success stories that can be attributed to the Flock system. Some incidents may not seem significant to everyone, but to the victims of these crimes, they matter. Every victim deserves to have their case taken seriously, and bringing an investigation to a successful conclusion can provide answers, accountability, and a sense of closure. The Grandville Police Department is grateful to the citizens and their support in our mission.

Fraud August 2026
Flock assisted officers in locating a theft suspect from a local retailer. The suspect was located and subsequently arrested in the area of 44th and I196 after committing the crime.

Larceny August 2026
Flock assisted officers in locating a suspect who stole an employee’s bicycle from a local business. The suspect damaged the bicycles lock, stole the bicycle, then left in a vehicle. The suspect was eventually identified, located, and the bicycle was recovered. The suspect was charged with larceny.

Larceny July 2026
A resident reported that an unknown subject stole their pet cat. A suspect vehicle description was captured on the owner’s security camera. Detectives were able to locate a possible suspect vehicle on several area Flock cameras. A plate was obtained and the cat was recovered and returned to their rightful owner.

Stolen Vehicle Recoveries July 2026
In a 12-hour shift, two stolen vehicles were located and recovered in the Grandville area thanks to Flock.

Fraud November 2025
A resident reported that someone had stolen her credit card and made multiple fraudulent transactions resulting in several police reports from multiple jurisdictions. A suspect vehicle description was obtained, and officers were able to utilize Flock in locating the vehicle on area Flock cameras. A suspect was developed and found to have several warrants including a felony warrant for fraud. The suspect was later located and arrested.

MDOP with Explosive Device December 2024
A suspect had thrown an explosive device from a vehicle. The device exploded, causing thousands of dollars’ worth of damage to several vehicles. A partial suspect vehicle description was obtained from residential security cameras. Officers located the suspect vehicle on nearby Flock cameras. Two suspects were eventually charged with destruction of property using an explosive device and with using explosives.

Fraud June of 2024
A victim reported that her purse and wallet were stolen in another jurisdiction. Several thousand dollars’ worth of fraudulent transactions were found to have occurred in Grandville. Grandville PD had not yet had Flock cameras installed at the time of the complaint, but detectives had access to the system since Flock was approved. Detectives were able to develop a suspect vehicle description by being allowed to view a nearby business security camera. Detectives utilized the Flock system and located the suspect vehicle on area cameras. A suspect was developed and felony warrants were later obtained.

Policy & Trust

What's Detected

License plate numbers, Vehicle make, Vehicle model, Vehicle color, Vehicle body style (SUV, pickup, sedan, etc.)

What's Not Detected

Facial recognition, People, Gender, Race, and any audio.

Acceptable Use Policy

Flock License Plate Reader (LPR) technology shall be used solely for legitimate law enforcement and public safety purposes. Users shall only access LPR data in connection with an authorized investigation, intelligence function, or public safety objective. Personal, political, or discriminatory use of the system is strictly prohibited. All system activity is subject to audit and review, and violations may result in disciplinary action, termination of access privileges, or criminal prosecution. Data is owned by the Grandville Police Department and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Prohibited Uses

The Grandville Police Department does not use the system for immigration enforcement, traffic enforcement, harassing or intimidation, usage based on a protected class (i.e. race, sex, religion), or personal use.

Access Policy

Authorized users may access the system only when performing official duties, supporting a criminal investigation, intelligence function, traffic safety initiative, missing person investigation, other legitimate public safety purpose, or when conducting activities authorized by department policy and applicable law.

Hotlist Policy

Vehicles may be entered into the Flock LPR hotlist when they are reasonably believed to be associated with an active criminal investigation and when the entry serves a legitimate law enforcement purpose. Investigative entries must be supported by a documented case number, intelligence file, or other official investigative record. All hotlist hits are required to be human verified prior to any enforcement action. Investigative hotlist entries shall be reviewed periodically and removed when the investigative need no longer exists.

Myth vs. Fact
Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
