Overview

The Lowell Police Department utilizes Flock Safety Automated License Plate Recognition (ALPR) technology as an investigative and public safety tool to assist officers in preventing, identifying, and investigating criminal activity within the City of Lowell. Flock Safety cameras provide officers with vehicle-related information that can be especially valuable when a vehicle is associated with a reported crime, stolen vehicle, wanted person, missing or endangered person, or other legitimate law enforcement investigation. The system supplements traditional police work by giving officers another resource to quickly identify vehicles of interest and develop investigative leads. This is particularly valuable for a smaller police department where staffing and available resources can be limited and officers must efficiently respond to a wide variety of calls for service and investigations.

The Lowell Police Department's purpose in using Flock cameras is public safety and legitimate law enforcement investigations—not the routine monitoring of residents or their daily activities. The technology is intended to make officers more effective by providing relevant vehicle information when there is a legitimate law enforcement need. Access to the system is limited to authorized personnel and used in accordance with department policy, applicable law, and established retention and auditing requirements. Searches have a legitimate law enforcement purpose. All searches must be linked to a criminal complaint number and a documented law enforcement reason.

Ultimately, Flock Safety is one component of the Lowell Police Department's overall approach to providing effective and responsible police services. It does not replace officers, community relationships, sound investigative practices, or independent decision-making. Instead, it provides officers with an additional tool that can help them respond more quickly to serious incidents, identify vehicles connected to criminal activity, develop investigative leads, recover stolen property, locate individuals who may be in danger, and work more effectively with surrounding law enforcement agencies. When used within appropriate policy and legal safeguards, the technology can improve investigative efficiency and enhance public safety while maintaining responsible oversight of how law enforcement technology is used in the Lowell community.

Policy & Trust

What's Detected

License Plates, Vehicles

What's Not Detected

• Photographs or facial recognition of pedestrians, drivers, or anyone in the vehicle - Flock cameras are designed to capture vehicles and license plates, not people
• Recording of any audio - there are no microphones for audio surveillance
• Continually track a person or vehicle - images of the vehicles are captured at precise locations rather than GPS style tracking
• Recognize race, gender, or personal identity of vehicle occupants
• Predict crimes automatically or determine criminal activity
• Run every plate automatically through LEIN/NCIC
• Capture images inside homes or buildings


Acceptable Use Policy

Data is used for official law enforcement purposes only and is restricted to public safety-related missions of the agency. Only trained sworn law enforcement officers authorized by the police department may access ALPR systems. The Data is owned by Lowell MI PD and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Prohibited Use

• Immigration enforcement
• Personal use
• Traffic enforcement
• Harassment or intimidation of any individual or group, based solely on a protected class (i.e. race, religion, gender, ethnicity, nationality, sexual orientation, political affiliation, disability, or other classification protected by law).
• Identifying any individual or group in the exercise of First Amendment rights, unless only for the protection of those persons


Access Policy

All system access requires a valid reason and is stored indefinitely.

Hotlist Policy

Hotlist hits are required to be human verified prior to action.

Myth vs. Fact
Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
