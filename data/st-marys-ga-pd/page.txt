Overview

St. Marys GA PD uses Flock Safety technology to capture objective evidence without compromising on individual privacy. St. Marys GA PD utilizes retroactive search to solve crimes after they've occurred. Additionally, St. Marys GA PD utilizes real time alerting of hotlist vehicles to capture wanted criminals. In an effort to ensure proper usage and guardrails are in place, they have made the below policies and usage statistics available to the public.

Armed Robbery

September 2, 2026
St. Marys, GA: Armed Robbery
On August 18, 2026, officers with the St. Marys Police Department responded to Kings Check Cashing following a reported armed robbery. The investigation quickly expanded into a multi-agency response led by the St. Marys Police Department Criminal Investigation Division in search of the suspect wanted in connection.
A preliminary investigation determined that the armed suspect approached the individuals outside of the business, displayed a handgun, and demanded money. A struggle occurred during the robbery before the suspect fled the scene on a scooter. Minor injuries were reported, and no property damage resulted from the incident. Investigators subsequently determined that the suspect was picked up by the occupant of a white vehicle, which then fled the area.
Detectives and crime analysis personnel developed and corroborated investigative leads that identified the vehicle used to assist in the armed robbery and Ronell Campbell as the suspect. Detectives determined Campbell had fled the State of Georgia and traveled to South Florida, where he was apprehended though a coordinated effort with local and federal law enforcement.
Ronell Campbell was taken into custody in Miami Gardens, Florida on August 20th. Campbell is charged with one count of Armed Robbery, Possession of Firearm by Convicted Felon, Possession of a Firearm in the Commission of a Felony, Conspiracy to Commit a Crime, and three counts of Aggravated Assault. Campbell is currently incarcerated and awaiting extradition.
“We understand the concern a violent incident like this creates for the people who live and work in our community. From the moment this incident was reported, our detectives worked relentlessly with our local, state, and federal partners to identify and locate the person responsible,” Chief James Galloway stated. “This case is an example of why those partnerships matter – investigations do not stop at jurisdictional or state lines.”
The St. Marys Police Department would like to recognize the detectives, analysts, officers, deputies, special agents and partner agencies whose combined efforts contributed to the identification, location, and apprehension of the suspect.

Acceptable Use Policy

Data is used for law enforcement purposes only. Data is owned by St. Marys GA PD and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Prohibited Uses

Immigration enforcement, traffic enforcement, harassment or intimidation, usage based solely on a protected class (i.e. race, sex, religion), Personal use.

All system access requires a valid reason and is stored indefinitely.

Myth vs. Fact
Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
